
# Phishing Prevention and Awareness Guidelines

## Introduction

Phishing attacks are used by cybercriminals to trick people into sharing sensitive information, clicking malicious links or downloading harmful attachments.

The following guidelines explain how individuals and organisations can protect themselves against phishing attacks.

## 1. Verify Email Senders

- Check the sender's email address carefully.
- Look for misspelled or unfamiliar domain names.
- Be cautious of emails pretending to come from trusted organisations.
- Contact the organisation through its official communication channels when unsure.

## 2. Avoid Suspicious Links

- Do not click links from unknown or unexpected senders.
- Check website addresses carefully before entering information.
- Visit official websites by typing their known addresses directly into your browser.
- Never enter passwords or banking details on unfamiliar websites.

## 3. Be Careful With Attachments

- Avoid opening unexpected attachments.
- Be cautious of unfamiliar files and documents.
- Use updated antivirus software.
- Report suspicious attachments to the appropriate security team.

## 4. Protect Personal Information

- Never share passwords through email.
- Do not provide banking information to unverified senders.
- Use strong and unique passwords.
- Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).

## 5. Recognise Urgent Messages

- Be careful of emails threatening account suspension.
- Watch out for messages demanding immediate payment.
- Do not allow pressure or fear to influence your decisions.
- Verify unusual requests independently.

## 6. Report Phishing Emails

- Report suspicious emails to your organisation's IT or security department.
- Follow your email provider's phishing reporting process.
- Do not forward suspicious links to other people.
- Delete confirmed phishing emails according to your organisation's procedures.

## 7. Security Awareness Training

Organisations should educate employees about phishing techniques.

Regular awareness training can help users recognise suspicious emails and respond safely to cyber threats.

## Conclusion

Phishing prevention requires awareness, careful communication and good cybersecurity practices.

By verifying email senders, avoiding suspicious links, protecting personal information and reporting suspicious messages, individuals and organisations can reduce their risk of phishing attacks.
