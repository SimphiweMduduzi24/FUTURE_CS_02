
# Phishing Email Investigation Results

## Introduction
This document presents the analysis of three sample emails to identify possible phishing attempts and suspicious email characteristics.

The investigation focuses on sender addresses, subject lines, email content, suspicious links and social engineering techniques.

---

## Email 1: Bank Account Alert

**Classification:** Phishing

**Sender:** security-alert@bank-example.invalid

**Subject:** URGENT! Your Bank Account Will Be Blocked!

### Identified Indicators
1. Urgent language is used to pressure the recipient.
2. The email threatens to suspend the bank account.
3. It asks the recipient to verify sensitive banking information.
4. It contains an unfamiliar verification link.

### Risk Level
High

### Recommended Action
Do not click the link or provide personal information. Contact the bank through its official website or telephone number.

---

## Email 2: Job Selection Notification

**Classification:** Suspicious

**Sender:** recruitment-team@jobs-example.invalid

**Subject:** Congratulations! You Have Been Selected!

### Identified Indicators
1. The recipient receives an unexpected job selection message.
2. The sender does not clearly identify a legitimate company.
3. The email requests personal information through an unfamiliar link.
4. It encourages the recipient to respond quickly.

### Risk Level
Medium

### Recommended Action
Verify the recruitment company independently before sharing personal information. Do not open unfamiliar links.

---

## Email 3: Examination Timetable

**Classification:** Appears Safe

**Sender:** student-services@university.example

**Subject:** Examination Timetable Available

### Identified Indicators
1. The message provides normal university-related information.
2. It does not request passwords or banking details.
3. It directs students to their usual university portal.
4. It recommends using official communication channels.

### Risk Level
Low (provisional)

### Recommended Action
Access the university portal using its known official address. Verify the sender before trusting the message.

---

## Conclusion

The investigation identified different characteristics associated with phishing and legitimate-looking emails.

Email 1 displayed strong phishing indicators, including threats and urgent requests for sensitive information.

Email 2 contained suspicious characteristics that require further verification.

Email 3 appeared safe based on its content, but its sender identity has not been independently verified.

This investigation demonstrates the importance of examining email content, sender information and links before trusting an email.

**Note:** These are fictional training samples. The classifications are preliminary and are based on the information provided in the sample messages, not verified email headers or live domain investigations.

## Comparative Analysis of Email Samples
